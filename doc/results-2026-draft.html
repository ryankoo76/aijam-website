Grand Prix · United Arab Emirates

SafeMama AI

Category Healthcare  ·  Entry no. 2026-0117
Problem

In rural Sub-Saharan Africa, the warning signs of a dangerous pregnancy arrive days before the emergency does — but the clinic is hours away and most mothers do not own a smartphone.

Solution

An SMS and voice screening line. A mother writes or speaks in her own words; a language model matches what she says against the WHO danger-sign list and routes urgent cases to the nearest health worker. No app, no data plan, no smartphone.

Why the judges chose it

Every year a handful of entries solve a real problem with technology the user cannot actually reach. SafeMama went the other way: it starts from the phone that is already in the mother's hand and asks how far intelligence can travel down a text message. The judging panel scored it highest on Impact and Feasibility, and it was the only project in the top ten that required no new hardware anywhere in the chain.

The AI does two jobs. First it reads a mother's free-text message. Then it matches it against the WHO list of danger signs — and it never decides on its own what happens next. From the team's submission

Special Awards

Given outside the category structure, for work that stood out on one dimension above all others.

Best AI Application
MedGuard
Vietnam · Healthcare
Three separate AI layers, each doing a job the others could not: computer vision reads the physical package, OCR lifts the printed batch data, and a language model reconciles both against live registry records. The judges singled it out for knowing exactly where the model ends and the database begins.
Best Social Impact
Adaptive Communication Resilience
India · Social Good
When a disaster overloads the network, this system reads signal strength, latency and surviving infrastructure in real time and re-routes communication through whatever is still standing. Built for the hours when everything else has stopped working.
Best Storytelling
Dyslexia Eye-Tracking Screen
United States · Healthcare
The clearest thirty seconds in the field. The team explained not only what they built but what failed first — and why an unstable tracker looks identical to a real eye movement. Honest engineering, told plainly.

How Entries Were Judged

4 criteria · equal weight

The same four criteria published before entries opened. Every project was scored independently on each.

01

Clarity

Could a stranger understand the idea, the build and the result in thirty seconds — without jargon standing in for explanation?

02

Novelty

Did the team approach the problem from an angle the rest of the field did not, or combine known parts in a way that was genuinely new?

03

Impact

Who is helped, how many of them, and by how much — stated in numbers the team could defend.

04

Feasibility

Could this be built and reach the people it is for, with the constraints the team already knows about?
